Issue 01 / Summer Service

Summer Is Here. Prep the Bike for Sunset Rides.

You walk toward the bike while the heat is still lifting off the pavement. Softer light, cleaner shadows, a road that stops looking like a commute and starts looking like an invitation. This is the hour motorcycles make the most sense. You are not escaping the day. You are finishing it right.

The sunset ride looks effortless from the outside, but the bike does not feel it as a postcard. It feels the hot start, the traffic crawl, the sudden throttle, the brake squeeze at the last yellow, the fan cycling, the chain load, the battery demand, and every small weakness you hoped would stay quiet.

You do not prep the bike because you fear the ride. You prep it because you respect it.

Start with the tires. Pressure cold, tread read like evidence, sidewalls checked, wear patterns honest. A cupping front feels vague in a lean. A squared rear fights smooth corners. Old rubber does not get safer at sunset — it just gets prettier. Then heat: coolant level, fan operation, the radiator clear, the temperature behavior understood. A bike that runs warm is not always broken. A bike that suddenly runs hotter than usual is talking. Listen before it shouts.

Brakes get the same respect. Squeeze the lever and notice whether it is firm, soft, spongy, pulsing, or late. Look at the pads and the fluid. If you cannot remember the last time the brakes felt perfect, that is already an answer. The chain and clutch tell you how mood becomes motion: a dry chain, a tight spot, worn sprockets, or hard shifting turns a clean run into a mechanical argument.

The fastest way to catch a problem is to notice the first difference, not the final failure. Did it sit too long? Did the battery struggle? Did the idle change? Did the fan run longer? When you feel that first difference, send it. That is the whole point of having a shop you trust on the other end of a text.
